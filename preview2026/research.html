Research

My research focuses on labor economics, with particular emphasis on intergenerational effects of economic shocks and labor market dynamics.

Job Market Paper

"Intergenerational Effects of Local Labor Market Shocks: Evidence from Plant Closures"

Working Paper November 2025

Abstract

This paper examines the long-term effects of parental job displacement on children's educational and labor market outcomes. Using administrative data linked across generations and leveraging mass layoffs for identification, I find that parental job loss during critical developmental periods leads to significant reductions in children's lifetime earnings. The effects are particularly pronounced for families with limited access to social insurance programs. I develop a dynamic model of human capital investment to decompose the mechanisms and find that credit constraints account for 40% of the effect, while changes in parental time investment explain an additional 30%.

"The Minimum Wage and the Gig Economy: Evidence from Seattle"

with Jane Smith and Robert Johnson August 2025

We study the impact of Seattle's minimum wage increase on the growth of gig economy participation. Using a difference-in-differences design and novel administrative data from platform companies, we find that the minimum wage increase led to a 15% increase in gig economy participation among affected workers.

"Union Decline and Rising Income Inequality: A Quantitative Assessment"

May 2025 R&R at Journal of Labor Economics

This paper quantifies the contribution of declining unionization to rising income inequality in the United States. I develop a structural model of union formation and wage determination, and use it to conduct counterfactual simulations. The results suggest that union decline can explain 20-30% of the increase in wage inequality since 1980.
